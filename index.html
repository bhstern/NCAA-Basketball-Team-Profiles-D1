About This Project

This started as an open-ended exploration. I was watching games every week, taking detailed notes, and I wanted to know if what the data said matched what my eyes were telling me. That question eventually turned into this site.

I built the entire thing from scratch — Python for the data pipeline and variable engineering, HTML and JavaScript for the front end, deployed on GitHub Pages. The platform covers all 368 D1 programs across 11 seasons (2016–2026) and roughly 53,000 individual player-seasons. The goal was never to just surface more stats. It was to build something a coaching staff could actually sit down with — to understand their own identity, prep for opponents, evaluate the portal, and make faster decisions. This is a proof of concept built entirely on public data. I know the real value multiplies with access to tools like Synergy Sports and Second Spectrum.

What’s in the Platform

Team Profile — Full team analytics for any D1 program, any season. Efficiency ratings, shot profile, Four Factors, custom PPP metrics, and a complete roster breakdown grouped by rotation tier. Every stat includes D1-wide percentiles, conference rank, and power/mid-major context.

Conference Context — How a team stacks up within its own conference across every key metric. Useful for understanding relative standing and preparing for conference opponents.

Player Explorer — Filter and sort 4,000+ players per season across five stat categories — overview, advanced, shooting, per-40, and contextual deltas. Six percentile contexts (national, conference, power/mid-major, position, and position within conference or subgroup). Threshold filters let you build any player profile you’re looking for.

Historical Trends — Track how a program has evolved over time across any metric. Useful for understanding roster construction patterns and long-term program identity.

Head to Head — Direct comparison of two teams across every metric, side by side.

Next up: individual player profiles, transfer portal decision support, and eventually clustering and similarity models with a long-term goal of building predictive tools on the player development side.

Data Sources & Citation

Raw team and player statistics, adjusted efficiencies, roster metrics, and shooting splits were sourced from Bart Torvik’s T-Rank database (barttorvik.com). All feature engineering — including shot profile construction, per-possession estimates, expected PPP (xPPP), shotmaking residuals, contextual delta metrics, percentile systems, and rotation tier classifications — was developed independently as part of this analytics pipeline.

Torvik, Bart. T-Rank College Basketball Statistics. barttorvik.com.

What Comes from Torvik

Adjusted efficiencies (adj_oe, adj_de, adj_em), tempo, Four Factors, roster metrics (height, experience, talent, SOS), raw shooting splits by shot type, and all player-level stats (BPM, PRPG, ORtg, DRtg, PPP Used, TS%, eFG%, shot attempt counts, rate stats, per-game box score, recruit rank, role, class, hometown) are sourced directly from T-Rank.

What Was Engineered Independently

Shooting percentages and attempt rates derived from counting stats, per-game volume stats, per-40 stats, age, position classification, recruit score rescaling, and height formatting were engineered from Torvik inputs.

The following were built entirely from scratch: combined rim metrics (off/def_rim_rate, off/def_rim_fg), the PPP system (ppp_off, ppp_def, ppp_diff), expected PPP (xPPP_off, xPPP_def) based on shot distribution multiplied by league-average shot values by year, shotmaking residuals (ppp_residual_off, ppp_residual_def), residual differential, TOV Sensitivity, Foul Sensitivity, all contextual delta metrics (vs. team, conference, and power/mid-major for ORtg, DRtg, TS%, eFG%, rim rate, and 3PT rate), the rotation tier system (mpg_tier), years_in_d1, and the full six-context percentile system (national, conference, power/mid, position, position+conference, position+power/mid).

Dataset Scope

11 seasons of D1 college basketball (2016–2026). Approximately 3,900 team-season records and 53,000 player-season records. Updated through the 2025–26 full season.
